Product & Brand Design — 2025

GARTEN & ICH

A print-on-demand garden brand, designed start to finish by Paolo Pedroso — one running joke, five product drops, and a mark that holds up on a t-shirt, a mug, and a mailer box.

RoleBrand & Product Design
ScopeIllustration, Identity, Packaging
Drops05, from Rake Puns to Sunflowers

Drop 01 — Best seller

One mark, every surface.

These visuals work because the identity stays put across a shirt, a mug, a tote, and the box it ships in. Same rake, same type, same three colors — recognizable before anyone reads the label.

Rechenzentrum — a pun that only lands in German: literally "data center," drawn as a row of garden rakes.

Drop 02 — Top seller

Charm that still does its job.

Whether it's a cozy mug or a canvas tote, every piece is built to bring a little warmth into an everyday routine — a small, well-drawn celebration of loving your garden.

Igel & Löwenzahn — a hedgehog making a wish on a dandelion, inked by hand.

Drop 03 — Top seller

Color decides the mood first.

A palette sets the emotional temperature before a single word gets read. It keeps the line consistent, makes each piece recognizable at a glance, and points the eye exactly where it should go.

Gänseblümchen — daisies, built around one four-color palette across bags, mugs, and packaging.

Drop 04 — Top seller

Less on the page, more of it seen.

Minimalism and white space aren't empty — they're what lets the sunflower, the type, and the call to action actually stand out and get noticed instead of competing for attention.

Sonnenblume — one sunflower, one warm neutral palette, four different surfaces.

Drop 05 — Top seller

Say it loud, say it once.

Impact is what makes a design work at all — it grabs attention fast, lands the joke in one line, and stays with you well after you've scrolled past it.

Hühner-Flüsterer — "the chicken whisperer," in retro type stripes across a coop's worth of merch.
